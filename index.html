3 Workforce Risks Hiding in Your HR Data

Prepared by: Girija S. | People Analytics Target Audience: VP of Human Resources, Executive Committee Dataset: IBM Attrition Study (1,470 records)

The Financial Problem

Replacing an employee is a severe financial leakage, costing between 50% and 200% of their annual salary in recruitment, training, ramp-up time, and lost institutional knowledge. Across our organization, 237 employees departed over this review cycle—a 16.1% attrition rate. Estimating a highly conservative replacement cost of $15,000 per head, this turnover is a $3.5 million baseline cost. This leakage is not uniform; it's highly clustered. Targeted, structural policy shifts—rather than generic broad engagement campaigns—represent the highest-ROI solution.

Key Workforce Risks & Findings

01 Overtime is a 3x Attrition Multiplier

Employees working overtime leave at a rate of 30.5%, compared to just 10.4% for those who do not work overtime. In organizational metrics, overtime is often misconstrued as engagement; the data shows it is a burnout fuse. Caps or triggers on chronic overtime are highly actionable levers.

02 The Sales Org is Losing 1 in 5 Staff Members

Sales department attrition is 20.6%, nearly double the Research & Development baseline rate (13.8%). Specifically, entry-level Sales Representatives face a catastrophic 39.8% attrition rate, driven by a combination of low entry pay (average $2,626/mo) and demanding workloads.

03 Entry-Level Pay Depressions Drive Out talent

Employees earning under $3,000/month leave at twice the rate of employees earning over $10,000/month. The median income of departing employees is roughly **$2,000/month lower** than those retained. A compensation adjustment program targeted at the lowest-paid critical operational roles would pay for itself in saved replacement costs.

Proposed Recommendations

1. Implement an Overtime "Circuit Breaker"

Setup automated flags in the HRIS/timekeeping system when an employee exceeds overtime thresholds for 3 consecutive weeks. This triggers an immediate operational review of manager resource allocations before resignation letters arrive. Cost: $0.

2. Compensation Adjustments for Bottom-Quartile Roles

Conduct a targeted compensation review for entry Sales Representatives and Laboratory Technicians (both carrying >23% churn). A modest 5-8% market alignment adjustment represents a fraction of the cost required to continually source and train their replacements.

3. Build a High-Risk Flight Warning Filter

Instead of relying on complex machine learning, setup simple queries filtering employees who have: Overtime = Yes AND Job Satisfaction <= 2 AND Tenure <= 3 years AND Stock Options = 0. Our database shows this cohort has a 60.6% attrition rate. Managers should prioritize direct retention reviews (stay interviews) for flagged employees.

Tableau Packaging & Layout Guide

Instructions and specs to build an interactive mirror of this analysis inside Tableau Desktop.

1

Connect Data

Import the cleaned CSV `output/hr_cleaned.csv` into Tableau. Force `AttritionFlag` to be a Dimension, and assign the 1-4 scale fields (e.g., JobSatisfaction) as Dimensions/Calculated segments.

2

Configure Color Palette

Add the custom color palette preference in your `Preferences.tps` file: Use `#1B2A4A` (Dark Navy), `#E05C5C` (Coral), and `#4A90D9` (Steel Blue).

3

Calculated Fields

Create key measures like Attrition Rate = SUM([AttritionFlag]) / COUNT([AttritionFlag]) and Avg Monthly Income = AVG([MonthlyIncome]).
